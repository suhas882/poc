# Public Records AI Search demo pitch

## Two-minute outline

**0:00–0:20 | The need**

Washington agencies have five business days to make an initial response to a public-records request. JLARC's 2024 agency-reported data showed an average of 24 days to close a request, with 57% closed within five days. Those are statewide figures, not Bellevue's baseline.

**0:20–0:55 | One request, two searches**

Use the invented request: “Please send records about the road closure on Cedar Avenue while utility crews did their work.” Show the plain keyword results, then synonym-expanded results for “lane closure,” “right-of-way work,” and “detour.” Explain that every record and label in this demo is synthetic.

**0:55–1:25 | Staff review**

Show the editable plan, candidate ranking, matching passage, explanation, and relevant/not relevant/unsure controls. The interface helps find and prioritize records. Staff make all legal decisions.

**1:25–1:45 | Search record**

Show the exportable search log and explain that the demo has no city connectors or live records.

**1:45–2:00 | Close**

“Staff decide. The tool finds and organizes candidates.” State that any future pilot is subject to agency requirements, approved data access, and agreed scope.

## Recording cautions

- Clearly identify the corpus as invented synthetic data.
- Do not present synthetic benchmark results as Bellevue performance.
- Any performance figures are target outcomes to be validated during discovery.
- Do not claim an embedding-based hybrid pipeline, a completed SureAccess scan, or a completed reviewer sign-off.
- The JLARC data is agency-reported and statewide. It is not Bellevue's baseline.
